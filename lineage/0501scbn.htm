501st Signal Battalion

"The Voice of the Eagle" — the signal battalion of the 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault). It carried the division's communications through the opening year of Operation Iraqi Freedom under Lt. Col. Welton Chase, Jr., and was inactivated in 2004 when the 101st converted to the Army's modular brigade structure. On 5 June 2026, at McAuliffe Hall, Fort Campbell, Kentucky, the division inactivated the 21st Brigade Engineer Battalion and activated the 501st Division Signal Battalion in a single ceremony, with LTC Brett Gilbert taking command from LTC Tucker Hughes. Veterans of every era back to Vietnam attended. Read the ceremony program.

The battalion's standing mission was to deploy anywhere in the world within 36 hours and establish and sustain the communications systems of the 101st Airborne Division, working through a headquarters company and companies A, B, and C. In 2003 that mission became the division's deep thrust into Iraq.

The network Chase's battalion put up had two layers. Single-channel FM, tactical satellite, and HF/UHF radio tied every command post together — the form of communication the division's planners called the most critical for an air assault — while Mobile Subscriber Equipment carried voice, data, and a common operating picture. Three node centers provided the backbone, five multichannel satellite terminals reached beyond line of sight, and smaller extension nodes followed the aviation brigades and the C2 helicopters serving as flying command posts.

The division then settled into Mosul and northern Iraq for the rest of the year. Soldiers of B Company held a retransmission site on a mountaintop about ten miles east of Tall Afar for eight months, boosting the link between convoys on Highway 1 and their headquarters. In late September, at the division's main command post in Mosul, the 501st joined the 101st's three infantry brigades in reviving the division's World War II helmet emblem — the old box and tick marks the battalion had worn from Normandy to the end of the war in Europe, now with a lightning bolt added in the center. "We use this distinct patch to link our past to our present, and it offers a bridge to the future of the 101st Airborne," Chase said at the ceremony.

The battalion's cost was counted on November 1, 2003, when Spc. Maurice J. Johnson of Company C, of Levittown, Pennsylvania, was killed by an improvised explosive device in Mosul while serving as a liaison to the governor of Nineveh Province.

On January 22, 2004, at the 101st Airborne Division's headquarters in Mosul, Chase furled the battalion's colors in a transfer-of-authority ceremony, ending a yearlong deployment and handing the communications mission for northern Iraq to the Iowa National Guard's 234th Signal Battalion of Cedar Rapids, led by Lt. Col. Rusty Lingenfelter. The division returned to Fort Campbell, Kentucky, later that year; signal companies inside the new brigade special troops battalions absorbed the 501st's role, and the battalion was inactivated.

Lineage

Constituted 23 July 1918 in the National Army as the 626th Field Signal Battalion and assigned to the 101st Division

(101st Division only partially organized October-November 1918; demobilized 11 December 1918)

Reconstituted 24 June 1921 in the Organized Reserves as the 101st Signal Company and assigned to the 101st Division

Organized in October 1921 in Wisconsin

Withdrawn 15 August 1942 from the Organized Reserves and allotted to the Army of the United States; concurrently reorganized at Camp Claiborne, Louisiana, as the 101st Airborne Signal Company, an element of the 101st Airborne Division

Inactivated 30 November 1945 in France

Allotted 25 June 1948 to the Regular Army

Activated 6 July 1948 at Camp Breckinridge, Kentucky

Inactivated 1 April 1949 at Camp Breckinridge, Kentucky

Activated 25 August 1950 at Camp Breckinridge, Kentucky

Inactivated 1 December 1953 at Camp Breckinridge, Kentucky

Activated 15 May 1954 at Fort Jackson, South Carolina

Reorganized and redesignated 1 July 1956 as Headquarters, Headquarters and Service Detachment, 501st Airborne Signal Battalion, an element of the 101st Airborne Division (516th Signal Company [see ANNEX 1] and 299th Signal Company [see ANNEX 2] concurrently redesignated as the Operations Company and the Installation Company, 501st Airborne Signal Battalion, respectively, and activated at Fort Campbell, Kentucky)

Reorganized and redesignated 25 April 1957 as the 501st Signal Battalion (Operations and Installation Companies concurrently reorganized and redesignated as Companies A and B, respectively)

ANNEX 1

Constituted 11 May 1942 in the Army of the United States as the 246th Signal Operation Company

Activated 15 April 1943 at Camp Crowder, Missouri

Inactivated 30 November 1945 at Fort Monmouth, New Jersey

Redesignated 15 March 1949 as the 516th Signal Company

Activated 1 April 1949 in Austria

Allotted 20 February 1951 to the Regular Army

Inactivated 31 July 1955 in Austria

ANNEX 2

Constituted 8 December 1942 in the Army of the United States as the 299th Signal Installation Company

Activated 24 September 1943 at Camp Crowder, Missouri

Redesignated 26 May 1945 as the 299th Signal Service Company

Inactivated 21 March 1946 in France

Redesignated 16 February 1953 as the 299th Signal Company and allotted to the Regular Army

Activated 25 March 1953 at Camp Gordon, Georgia

Inactivated 27 June 1955 at Camp Gordon, Georgia
