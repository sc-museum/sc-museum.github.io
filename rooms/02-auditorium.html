This Month in History

Signal Corps anniversaries for the month, with a door into the room that tells each story.

October
Two October 4thsA Signal Corps pigeon became a legend in 1918. In 1957 Signal Corps antennas were the first in the U.S. government to record Sputnik.
Oct11867
Signal Training

Signaling Returns to West Point

On October 1, 1867, at Albert J. Myer's urging and with Ulysses S. Grant's approval, the U.S. Military Academy began teaching signaling and telegraphy again. The Naval Academy also taught Myer's system.

Oct11890
Weather

The weather service leaves the Army

On October 1, 1890, President Benjamin Harrison signed the law creating the Weather Bureau in the Department of Agriculture. For about twenty years Signal Corps soldiers had run the nation's weather service from posts across the country. The handover took effect on July 1, 1891.

Oct11937
Chiefs of Signal

Mauborgne Becomes Chief Signal Officer

On October 1, 1937, Col. Joseph O. Mauborgne became chief signal officer. An expert in cryptology and radio, he backed the Signal Intelligence Service work that broke Japan's Purple cipher three years later.

Oct11948
Camp Gordon

The Signal Corps Comes to Camp Gordon

On October 1, 1948, the Army activated a Signal Corps Training Center at Camp Gordon, Georgia. It began the post's long life as a home of the Signal Corps. The Military Police School had arrived the month before.

Oct11974
Fort Gordon

Home of the Signal Corps

On October 1, 1974, Fort Gordon became the U.S. Army Signal Center and Fort Gordon, the new "home of the Signal Corps." Three months earlier, on July 1, as the Army consolidated most of its signal training there, the post's Southeastern Signal School had been renamed the U.S. Army Signal School, and the school at Fort Monmouth became the Communications-Electronics School.

Oct12010
Cyber

Army Cyber Command Is Born

On October 1, 2010, U.S. Army Cyber Command was activated at Fort Belvoir, Virginia, with Maj. Gen. Rhett A. Hernandez in command. It moved its headquarters to Fort Gordon in 2020.

Oct31993
Fort Gordon

MSG Gary Gordon at Mogadishu

On October 3, 1993, in Mogadishu, Somalia, Master Sgt. Gary I. Gordon and Sgt. 1st Class Randall Shughart asked to be put on the ground to protect the crew of a downed Black Hawk. Both were killed, and both received the Medal of Honor. Since 2025 the post has been named Fort Gordon in his honor.

Oct41918
Signal Corps Pigeon Service

Cher Ami and the Lost Battalion

Cut off in the Argonne Forest and shelled by their own artillery, Maj. Charles Whittlesey's men sent a message by Signal Corps pigeon, and the battalion was relieved on the night of October 7. Tradition credits Cher Ami, who reached his loft badly wounded, with carrying it, though a Signal Corps memo of September 20, 1920 admitted there was no war record that he did. Wartime records called the bird a hen; a DNA test at the Smithsonian in 2021 showed Cher Ami was male. His mounted remains are at the Smithsonian (source).

Oct41957
Signal Corps Laboratories

Sputnik, heard first at Deal

When the Soviet Union launched Sputnik I, Signal Corps engineers at the Deal Test Area, a satellite campus of Fort Monmouth, New Jersey, were the first U.S. government team to detect and record its signals. They followed 273 orbits over about 500 hours, though no project had been set up for the job.

Oct61972
Signal Photography

The Army Pictorial Center Closes

On October 6, 1972, the Army Pictorial Center in Astoria, New York, was closed as an Army installation. The old Paramount studio had made Signal Corps training and information films since 1942.

Oct81909
Army Aviation

The first Army pilots learn to fly

On October 8, 1909, formal flight training began at College Park, Maryland, with Wilbur Wright teaching two Army lieutenants. On October 26 Lt. Frederic Humphreys flew solo, and Lt. Frank Lahm followed minutes later. On October 10, 1911, the Army tried dropping bombs from an airplane for the first time.

Oct161913
Army Aviation

The First Gold Aviator Badges

On October 16, 1913, the two original gold Military Aviator badges were presented to Capt. Charles Chandler and Lt. Thomas Milling. Badges went next to twelve more officers.

Oct181943
Signal Corps Pigeon Service

G.I. Joe Saves a Village

On October 18, 1943, the U.S. Army pigeon G.I. Joe carried a message that called off a planned Allied bombing of Calvi Vecchia, Italy, which British troops had just taken. He flew 20 miles in 20 minutes and arrived as the bombers were getting ready. In 1946 he received Britain's Dickin Medal.

Oct201944
World War II

The Signal Corps' Grand Fleet at Leyte

On October 20, 1944, Gen. Douglas MacArthur's forces landed on Leyte in the Philippines. Offshore were the Signal Corps' own communications ships, later called the Signal Corps' Grand Fleet. They carried MacArthur's 'I have returned' broadcast to the United States.

Oct241941
Chiefs of Signal

Olmstead Takes the Helm

On October 24, 1941, Dawson Olmstead officially became chief signal officer with the rank of major general, the fifteenth person to hold the post. Six weeks later the United States was at war.

Oct251866
Chiefs of Signal

Myer's Return Ordered

On October 25, 1866, President Andrew Johnson ordered Secretary of War Edwin Stanton to appoint Albert J. Myer chief signal officer under the new law that made the Signal Corps permanent. Stanton made the appointment five days later.

Oct261918
Code Talkers

Choctaw Voices on the Line

On October 26, 1918, during the Meuse-Argonne campaign, Col. A. W. Bloor of the 142d Infantry, 36th Division, used Choctaw soldiers to pass orders by telephone in their own language as two companies pulled back. German listeners could not understand it. The soldiers became known as the first code talkers.

Oct272023
Fort Gordon

Fort Gordon Becomes Fort Eisenhower

On October 27, 2023, at a ceremony on Barton Field, Fort Gordon was renamed Fort Eisenhower, for President Dwight D. Eisenhower. It was the last of nine Army posts renamed on the recommendation of the congressional Naming Commission. In 2025 the name Fort Gordon returned, honoring Master Sgt. Gary Gordon.

Also this monthAmerican Archives Month: the CECOM History Office archive holds more than a hundred years of Signal Corps history, from Greely's Arctic journal to World War I training-camp photographs.
November
Weather, Wings, and CyberNovember runs from the Signal Service's first telegraphed weather reports in 1870 to the first West Point cadets choosing the new Cyber Branch in 2014.
Nov11870
Signal Service Weather

The Army's Weather Network Goes Live

At 7:35 a.m. on November 1, 1870, Signal Service observer-sergeants at 24 stations took the first synchronized weather readings and sent them by telegraph to Washington, D.C. The stations stretched from Boston to Key West and west to Cheyenne in Wyoming Territory. The network, organized under Chief Signal Officer Albert J. Myer, began the Army's twenty years of national weather reporting.

Nov11918
World War I

Fake Radio Traffic Before the Meuse Attack

Before the American attack along the Meuse on November 1, 1918, the Signal Corps sent out false radio traffic to mislead the Germans about where the blow would fall. Ten days later the Armistice ended the war.

Nov11995
Fort Gordon

The Navy Comes to Fort Gordon

On November 1, 1995, the Naval Security Group Activity Fort Gordon was commissioned to provide Navy cryptologic sailors to the Gordon Regional Security Operations Center. Sailors have served on post ever since. In 2005 it became Navy Information Operations Command Georgia.

Nov71972
Vietnam

The 1st Signal Brigade Moves to Korea

On November 7, 1972, the headquarters of the 1st Signal Brigade left Vietnam and moved its colors to Korea. At its peak the brigade had been the largest signal command of the war. It has served in Korea ever since.

Nov81942
World War II

Operation TORCH Tests the Signal Corps

On November 8, 1942, British and American forces landed in North Africa in Operation TORCH, which the Army's branch history calls the Signal Corps' "first major test of the war." Naval gunfire knocked out many shipboard radios, and the heavy SCR-299 radio trucks were stowed so deep in the holds that only one came ashore in time. Two British communications ships helped fill the gap.

Nov81966
Vietnam / Medal of Honor

Capt. Euripides Rubio, Communications Officer

On November 8, 1966, during Operation ATTLEBORO in Tay Ninh Province, Vietnam, Capt. Euripides Rubio was communications officer of the 1st Battalion, 28th Infantry. Wounded three times, he took command of a rifle company, then carried a misplaced marking smoke grenade toward the enemy so the air strikes would fall on the enemy, not his men. He received the Medal of Honor posthumously.

Nov101863
Civil War

Myer Removed as Chief

On November 10, 1863, Secretary of War Edwin Stanton removed Albert J. Myer as chief signal officer, after a long fight over control of the Army's field telegraph. Myer was restored to the post in 1867.

Nov181912
Army Aviation in Augusta

Back to Augusta

On November 18, 1912, a nine-car special train left College Park, Maryland, carrying Capt. Charles Chandler, Lts. Lewis Graham and William Sherman, five airplanes and their crews back to Augusta, Georgia, for a second winter of flying. College Park closed that day.

Nov202014
Cyber Branch

First Cadets Branch Cyber

On November 20, 2014, at Branch Night at the U.S. Military Academy, 15 cadets of the Class of 2015 received assignments as Cyber officers (17A). They were the first West Point cadets to branch directly into the new Cyber Branch, created that September 1. The next night they met Army Cyber Command and Cyber School leaders, including Cyber School Commandant Col. Jennifer Buckner.

Nov221963
Signal Photography

A Signal Corps Camera on Air Force One

On November 22, 1963, Capt. Cecil Stoughton of the Army Signal Corps, assigned to the White House, was the only photographer aboard Air Force One when Lyndon B. Johnson took the oath of office after President Kennedy's assassination. His picture became one of the best-known photographs of the century.

Nov231977
Hello Girls

Veterans at Last

On November 23, 1977, President Jimmy Carter signed the G.I. Bill Improvement Act. It let the Defense Department recognize certain civilian groups' wartime service as active military service, which opened the way for the Signal Corps' World War I telephone operators to be recognized as veterans. Few of them were still alive.

Nov241877
Coastal Signal Stations

Telegraph at the Wreck of the Huron

Early on November 24, 1877, the steamer Huron ran aground off Nags Head, North Carolina. Sgt. S. W. Naylor, the Signal Corps observer at Kitty Hawk, eight miles away, hurried to the scene and set up a telegraph station on the shore. It sent more than 550 messages through December 11. Only 34 of the 132 men aboard survived.

Nov291911
Army Aviation in Augusta

Army Fliers Winter in Augusta

On November 29, 1911, the Signal Corps aviation school from College Park, Maryland, arrived in Augusta, Georgia, to keep flying through the winter on the Barnes farm. That winter proved unusually harsh: heavy snow fell in January and February 1912. Pilots trained here included Henry H. "Hap" Arnold and Thomas DeW. Milling. The Signal Corps returned to the Augusta area in 1948 at Camp Gordon.

Nov292016
Cyber

Breaking Ground for Army Cyber's Headquarters

On November 29, 2016, Secretary of the Army Eric Fanning and Lt. Gen. Paul Nakasone broke ground at Fort Gordon for the new Army Cyber Command headquarters. The building, later named Fortitude Hall, was dedicated in September 2020.

Nov301967
Fort Gordon

Training Center Reorganized

On November 30, 1967, at the height of the Vietnam War, the Signal Corps Training Center at Fort Gordon was reorganized to coordinate advanced individual training.

Also this monthBeginning in November 1917, the Signal Corps recruited women from commercial telephone companies, plus bilingual volunteers, as telephone operators; they became the Signal Corps Female Telephone Operators Unit, better known as the "Hello Girls."
December
From Radar to OrbitSignal Corps radar spotted the attackers approaching Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, and seventeen years later, on December 18, 1958, the Corps launched the world's first communications satellite.
Dec31941
Alaska Communication System

Alaska's Cable Restored

On December 3, 1941, the reconditioned undersea cable between Seattle and Alaska went back into service, restoring secure communications with the mainland. Four days later came Pearl Harbor. The Signal Corps' Alaska Communication System became the backbone of wartime communications in the territory.

Dec41990
Signal Units

The 6th Signal Command

On December 4, 1990, the Army activated the 6th Signal Command at Fort Huachuca, Arizona, as Desert Shield forces gathered in the Persian Gulf.

Dec61907
Army Aviation

Selfridge Goes Up in a Kite

On December 6, 1907, during trials on a lake, Lt. Thomas Selfridge rose 168 feet in one of Alexander Graham Bell's man-carrying kites and stayed up seven minutes. He was working with Bell's Aerial Experiment Association.

Dec61941
Codebreaking

The Fourteen-Part Message

Early in the afternoon of December 6, 1941, the Army's Signal Intelligence Service began receiving a long message in fourteen parts from Tokyo to the Japanese embassy in Washington. Japan held back the last part until the next morning; it broke off talks with the United States.

Dec71911
Army Aviation in Augusta

Flying Begins in Augusta

On December 7, 1911, the Signal Corps aviation school began flying at Augusta, Georgia. Each officer made a flight in the Burgess-Wright, the first airplane put together after the trip south. The school stayed until April.

Dec71941
World War II / Radar

Radar Warning at Opana

At 7:02 a.m. on December 7, 1941, Signal Corps Pvts. Joseph L. Lockard and George A. Elliott, still running their radar at Opana on Oahu's northern tip after their watch ended, saw a large echo about 130 miles out. They telephoned the information center at Fort Shafter but were told to "Forget it." The attack on Pearl Harbor began at 7:55.

Dec81912
Army Aviation

A Flying School at North Island

On December 8, 1912, the Signal Corps opened its own flying school on North Island in San Diego Bay. Within a year it became the Signal Corps Aviation School, the Army's main flight school.

Dec81941
World War II

Amateur Radio Goes Silent

On December 8, 1941, the day after Pearl Harbor, the government closed all amateur radio stations, as it had in World War I. The president created the Office of Censorship a few days later.

Dec91941
Camp Gordon

Camp Gordon Opens for Business

Two days after Pearl Harbor, on December 9, 1941, camp commander Col. Herbert W. Schmid moved his small staff from a temporary office in Augusta's post office to the unfinished headquarters building at Camp Gordon. That month the 4th Infantry Division began moving in. The camp hosted three divisions in World War II and became a Signal Corps training center in 1948.

Dec131913
Army Aviation

The Signal Corps Aviation School

On December 13, 1913, the Army's air school at San Diego officially became the Signal Corps Aviation School, part of the Army school system. The Army let its lease at College Park lapse.

Dec151880
Chiefs of Signal

Hazen Takes Office

On December 15, 1880, Brig. Gen. William B. Hazen became the fourth chief signal officer, after Albert J. Myer's death. He led the Signal Corps through the Greely Expedition and its troubled rescue.

Dec161944
World War II

Holding the Lines in the Bulge

On December 16, 1944, German forces attacked through the Ardennes, opening the Battle of the Bulge. Though American units were caught by surprise, their communications held. Col. Garland C. Black, signal officer of Gen. Omar Bradley's 12th Army Group, had built alternate routes for key circuits, using French underground cables backed by open wire lines. Bradley never lost contact with his armies.

Dec181958
Satellites

SCORE: A Voice from Orbit

On December 18, 1958, the Signal Corps, with an Air Force Atlas rocket, launched Project SCORE, the world's first communications satellite. It showed that an orbiting relay could receive, store, and send on voice and coded signals. SCORE broadcast a taped Christmas greeting from President Eisenhower: "my voice is coming to you from a satellite circling in outer space." It was built to last only weeks.

Dec231907
Army Aviation

The Army Advertises for an Airplane

On December 23, 1907, the Signal Corps issued Specification No. 486, seeking bids for a "heavier-than-air flying machine." It had to carry two people, fly at least 40 miles per hour, and stay aloft for an hour. Bids were due February 1, 1908. The Wright brothers won the contract, and their machine became the Army's first airplane, Signal Corps No. 1.

Dec291941
Code Talkers

Comanche Code Talkers Reach Camp Gordon

On December 29, 1941, the 4th Signal Company of the 4th Infantry Division, including its Comanche code talkers, arrived at Camp Gordon, Georgia. The Comanche soldiers had built a code from their own language. In 1944 they landed on Utah Beach.

Dec311923
Chiefs of Signal

Squier Retires

On December 31, 1923, Maj. Gen. George O. Squier retired as chief signal officer. A scientist and inventor, he had led the Signal Corps through World War I and pushed early Army aviation.

Also this monthIn December 1936, Signal Corps engineers ran the first field test of their radar at the Newark, New Jersey, airport, where it detected an airplane seven miles away.
January
Moon Echo to CyberJanuary links the Signal Corps' 1946 radar echo from the Moon with Fort Gordon's 2014 start as the Army's Cyber Center of Excellence.
Jan11899
Spanish-American War

The Signal Corps Takes Over Cuba's Wires

On January 1, 1899, the Signal Corps took charge of the telegraph and telephone lines the Spanish government had run in Cuba. Col. Henry Dunwoody, chief signal officer of the new Division of Cuba, also oversaw the private companies Spain had licensed. One of his first steps was to separate the postal and telegraph services.

Jan11935
Chiefs of Signal

Allison Becomes Chief Signal Officer

Maj. Gen. James B. Allison became chief signal officer on January 1, 1935. During World War I he had commanded Signal Corps training camps at Monterey, Fort Leavenworth and Camp Meade, and he later led the Signal School. He served until the end of September 1937.

Jan71961
Fort Gordon

Eisenhower's Last Review

On January 7, 1961, days before leaving office, President Dwight D. Eisenhower reviewed troops at Fort Gordon. He called it his last military review and used it to say goodbye to the Army he had joined half a century earlier.

Jan91945
World War II

Signal Troops Land on Luzon

On January 9, 1945, General Douglas MacArthur's forces invaded Luzon in the Philippines. Naval fire-control parties of the 293d Joint Assault Signal Company went ashore to call in fire from the ships. The Luzon fight became the largest ground campaign of the Pacific war, and Brig. Gen. Spencer Akin's signal troops grew with it.

Jan101946
Signal Corps Laboratories

Project Diana Reaches the Moon

On January 10, 1946, a Signal Corps team at the Evans Signal Laboratory in New Jersey bounced a radar signal off the Moon and received its echo about 2.5 seconds later. Led by John H. DeWitt, and drawing on scientist Walter S. McAfee's calculations, the team used a modified SCR-271 radar. Project Diana showed that signals could pass through the ionosphere into space.

Jan151911
Army Aviation

First Live Bomb from a Wright Plane

On January 15, 1911, near Los Angeles, Lt. Myron Crissy of the Coast Artillery Corps dropped a live bomb of his own design from a Wright airplane flown by Philip O. Parmalee. It was the first time live bombs were dropped from a Wright plane. The Army was still testing what airplanes could do in war.

Jan161962
Signal Corps Organization

The End of the Technical Services

On January 16, 1962, President John F. Kennedy sent Congress a plan abolishing the Army's technical services, except for the Medical Department. The change took effect on February 17. The Office of the Chief Signal Officer survived it for two more years before it too was replaced in 1964.

Jan171991
Desert Storm

Desert Storm Begins

On January 17, 1991, after Iraq ignored the January 15 deadline to leave Kuwait, the United States and its allies began Operation DESERT STORM with air attacks on Iraq and Kuwait. Signal units had spent months building a theater network across Saudi Arabia. The ground war followed in late February.

Jan211959
Satellites

SCORE Falls Silent

On January 21, 1959, the SCORE satellite re-entered the atmosphere and burned up. Launched by the Signal Corps and the Air Force on December 18, 1958, it had relayed voice and coded messages until its batteries gave out after about twelve days. It was the world's first communications satellite.

Jan221944
World War II

The Walkie-Talkie Goes to War at Anzio

On January 22, 1944, the U.S. VI Corps landed behind German lines at Anzio, Italy. Signal planners used lessons from earlier landings, and the SCR-300 radio, better known as the walkie-talkie, saw its first combat use there.

Jan242014
Fort Gordon / Cyber

Fort Gordon Becomes the Cyber Center of Excellence

On January 24, 2014, the Army issued an order (EXORD 057-14) establishing the U.S. Army Cyber Center of Excellence and a Cyber School at Fort Gordon. The Signal Center of Excellence became the Cyber Center of Excellence, with the Signal and Cyber Schools under one two-star command. The Army's Cyber Branch followed on September 1, 2014.

Jan251912
Army Aviation in Augusta

Hap Arnold Climbs over Augusta

On January 25, 1912, at the Signal Corps winter flying school in Augusta, Georgia, Lt. Henry H. Arnold climbed to 4,764 feet. He broke his own Army altitude record. Snow and cold kept the school on the ground for much of that winter.

Jan311958
Satellites

Explorer I Reaches Orbit

On January 31, 1958, the United States launched its first satellite, Explorer I. Parts of its electronic equipment had been developed by the Signal Corps. Its instruments discovered the Van Allen radiation belt around the earth.

Also this monthOn January 4, 2016, the first Cyber Captains Career Course began at the Army Cyber School at Fort Gordon, with twenty officers starting the 26-week course.
February
Weather and WingsIn February 1870 Congress gave the Signal Corps a national weather service, and in February 1908 the Corps signed the Army's first airplane contract.
Feb11943
Codebreaking

Venona Begins at Arlington Hall

On February 1, 1943, Gene Grabeel, a former schoolteacher working for the Army's Signal Intelligence Service, began studying encrypted Soviet diplomatic messages at Arlington Hall, Virginia. The effort, later called Venona, ran until 1980. It exposed Soviet espionage in the United States.

Feb41881
Signal Corps founder

Fort Whipple Becomes Fort Myer

On February 4, 1881, the Army renamed Fort Whipple, Virginia, as Fort Myer to honor Albert J. Myer, the Army's first signal officer and founder of the Signal Corps. Signal training had been held at the post. Myer had died the previous August at age 51, soon after his promotion to brigadier general. As head of the weather service, he was popularly known as "Old Probabilities."

Feb51899
Medal of Honor

Up the Pole at Paco Bridge

On February 5, 1899, at Paco Bridge in Manila, 1st Lt. Charles E. Kilbourne of the Volunteer Signal Corps climbed a telegraph pole about 250 yards from the enemy. Under rapid fire, he repaired a broken wire and restored telegraph communication with the front. He received the Medal of Honor.

Feb81968
Fort Gordon

Last Signal OCS Class at Fort Gordon

On February 8, 1968, the 53 members of Class 04-68 took the oath as second lieutenants in Fort Gordon's Alexander Hall, ending the Signal Officer Candidate School. Activated at Fort Gordon in September 1965, the 23-week course had trained more than 2,000 officers. Many graduates soon carried heavy responsibilities in Vietnam, often beyond their rank.

Feb91870
Weather

Congress Creates a Weather Service

On February 9, 1870, President Ulysses S. Grant signed a joint resolution of Congress directing the Secretary of War to take weather observations at military stations and to warn the Great Lakes and seacoast of approaching storms by telegraph. Chief Signal Officer Albert J. Myer asked for the job, and the Signal Corps took on the work that grew into today's National Weather Service.

Feb91914
Army Aviation

Lt. Henry Post Lost over San Diego Bay

On February 9, 1914, Lt. Henry B. Post was killed when his Wright C seaplane crashed into San Diego Bay. He had been trying for an altitude record and had reached an unofficial 12,140 feet. Post Field, a World War I training field in Oklahoma, was named for him.

Feb101906
Leaders

Greely's Long Watch Ends

On February 10, 1906, President Theodore Roosevelt promoted Chief Signal Officer Adolphus W. Greely to major general, and the War Department sent him to command the Pacific Division at San Francisco. Greely had led the Signal Corps for nearly nineteen years, the longest of any chief signal officer, guiding it from the weather-service years into the electrical age.

Feb101908
Aviation

The Army Orders Its First Airplane

On February 10, 1908, the Signal Corps and Wilbur and Orville Wright signed the first formal Army airplane contract. Under Signal Corps Specification No. 486, the machine had to carry two people at about 40 miles per hour and stay in the air for at least an hour. The price was $25,000; a speed bonus later raised the payment to $30,000.

Feb121915
Army Aviation

The First Aviation Mechanicians

On February 12, 1915, twenty enlisted men of the Signal Corps Aviation Section passed their examination and were rated Aviation Mechanicians. The rating, created under the 1914 law that set up the Aviation Section, came with extra pay for skilled ground crews.

Feb131913
Chiefs of Signal

General Allen Retires

On February 13, 1913, Brig. Gen. James Allen stepped down as chief signal officer after seven years, having reached the mandatory retirement age of 64. Under Allen the Signal Corps had bought the Army's first airplane and started its first flying schools.

Feb171913
Army Aviation

Testing an Automatic Pilot

On February 17, 1913, at San Diego, Lt. Harold Geiger began test flights of the Army's first flying boat, Signal Corps No. 15, a Curtiss fitted with a Sperry automatic pilot. It was the first device of its kind the Signal Corps tested. The two-seat flying boat let an instructor sit beside the student.

Feb231912
Army Aviation

A New Rating: Military Aviator

On February 23, 1912, the War Department announced a new rating of Military Aviator, a tougher test of pilot skill than the civilian FAI license. The first three officers to earn it qualified that July.

Feb251913
Army Aviation in Augusta

First Cross-Country Landing from Augusta

On February 25, 1913, Capt. Charles Chandler flew 28 miles south from the Augusta flying school and landed in a field at Waynesboro, Georgia. It was the school's first flight that landed away from home. That evening he was told the school was moving to Texas City because of trouble with Mexico.

Also this monthIn February 1942 the Signal Corps bought Paramount's film studio in Astoria, Long Island, which opened that May as the Signal Corps Photographic Center.
March
A Corps Takes ShapeMarch marks both the 1863 law that created a Signal Corps for the Civil War and the 1956 order that made Camp Gordon the permanent Fort Gordon.
Mar11923
Signal Corps Organization

The War Department Message Center

On March 1, 1923, the War Department telegraph office and the Signal Corps' own telegraph office and radio station merged into the War Department Message Center. It grew out of the success of the Army's peacetime radio net.

Mar11964
Signal Corps Organization

Farewell to the Chief Signal Officer

On March 1, 1964, the Army replaced the Office of the Chief Signal Officer with the Office of the Chief of Communications-Electronics, part of the Army Staff. The post of chief signal officer, first held by Albert J. Myer in 1860, came to an end.

Mar21910
Army Aviation

Foulois Teaches Himself to Fly

On March 2, 1910, at Fort Sam Houston, Texas, Lt. Benjamin D. Foulois made his first solo flight in Signal Corps Airplane No. 1. He made his first solo, first landing and first crash all that day. He was largely self-taught, writing to the Wright brothers for advice.

Mar31863
Civil War

Lincoln Signs the Signal Corps Into Law

On March 3, 1863, President Abraham Lincoln signed a bill that organized a Signal Corps for the duration of the "present rebellion." Until then, Albert J. Myer had depended on officers and men detailed from other units. The law created a chief signal officer with the rank of colonel and required entrance examinations. Historian Rebecca Raines called it "truly a signal victory."

Mar51913
Aviation

From Augusta to the 1st Aero Squadron

In February 1913, trouble with Mexico led the Army to mobilize along the border, and Signal Corps aviators wintering at Augusta, Georgia, were called to Texas. On March 5, 1913, at Texas City, they were organized as the 1st Aero Squadron (Provisional), with nine airplanes, under Capt. Charles deForest Chandler. Augusta's winter flying school later lent its area to Camp Gordon's story.

Mar52012
Fort Gordon

NSA Opens the Georgia Cryptologic Center

On March 5, 2012, the National Security Agency opened its Georgia Cryptologic Center at Fort Gordon with a ribbon-cutting. The $286 million complex covers about 604,000 square feet and replaced a smaller center on post.

Mar61918
Women in the Signal Corps

First Women Operators Sail for France

On March 6, 1918, the first unit of 33 Signal Corps women telephone operators sailed for Europe aboard the Army transport Celtic, led by chief operator Grace Banker. Fluent in French and English, they began official duties in France on March 24 and ran American Expeditionary Forces switchboards in Paris, Chaumont, and seventy-five other French cities and towns.

Mar71906
Medal of Honor

Lt. Gordon Johnston at Bud Dajo

On March 7, 1906, during the assault on Mount Bud Dajo on Jolo in the Philippines, 1st Lt. Gordon Johnston of the Signal Corps volunteered to join the attack and was badly wounded. He received the Medal of Honor. A cavalry officer, he had been detailed to the Signal Corps against his wishes.

Mar121912
Army Aviation

A Flying School in the Philippines

On March 12, 1912, Lt. Frank P. Lahm, the Army's first certified pilot, opened the Philippine Air School at Fort William McKinley near Manila. He was on temporary aviation duty with the Signal Corps. His students there included Cpl. Vernon Burge, who became the Army's first enlisted pilot.

Mar151870
Signal Service Weather

Weather Duty Goes to the Signal Officer

On March 15, 1870, Secretary of War William Belknap gave the Army's new weather-reporting job to Chief Signal Officer Albert J. Myer. Congress had approved the service a month earlier. The first reports went out by telegraph that November.

Mar151916
Army Aviation

1st Aero Squadron Joins the Punitive Expedition

On March 15, 1916, the Signal Corps' 1st Aero Squadron reached Columbus, New Mexico, with eight Curtiss JN-3 airplanes, to join the hunt for Pancho Villa. It was the first U.S. aviation unit sent on a military campaign. Capt. Benjamin Foulois commanded.

Mar151973
Vietnam

The Last Signal Unit Leaves Vietnam

On March 15, 1973, the 39th Signal Battalion left Vietnam, almost eleven years after its first elements arrived. It was the first signal unit to arrive and the last to leave. The battalion took part in all seventeen campaigns of the war.

Mar171958
Satellites

Signal Corps Solar Cells in Orbit

On March 17, 1958, the Navy launched the Vanguard I satellite. It carried solar cells developed by the Signal Corps to keep its radio powered in space.

Mar211956
Fort Gordon

Camp Gordon Becomes Fort Gordon

On March 21, 1956, the Army redesignated Camp Gordon, Georgia, as Fort Gordon, making it a permanent installation. Opened as a World War II training camp near Augusta, the post became a Signal Corps training center in 1948, home of the Southeastern Signal School. It grew quickly during the Korean War, when the Army needed many more trained signal soldiers.

Mar231962
Vietnam

The First Signal Company in Vietnam

On March 23, 1962, the 232d Signal Company arrived in Vietnam. It was among the earliest Army signal units of the war.

Mar241976
Notable soldiers

First Black General of the Signal Corps

On March 24, 1976, Brig. Gen. Emmett Paige Jr. became the Signal Corps' first Black general officer. He had commanded the 361st Signal Battalion in Vietnam and was then leading the 11th Signal Group at Fort Huachuca, Arizona. Signal Officer Candidate School at Fort Gordon

On September 20, 1965, an order activated the Signal Officer Candidate School at Fort Gordon, creating the 1st Officer Candidate Battalion under Maj. John J. McManus. The program began with ten officers and no facilities of its own. The first class arrived that November. When the last class was commissioned in February 1968, the school had produced 2,210 second lieutenants during the Vietnam War era.

W. Hallett Greene Joins the Signal Corps

On September 26, 1884, W. Hallett Greene became the first Black soldier admitted to the Signal Corps. A new graduate of the College of the City of New York, he had been rejected by Chief Signal Officer William B. Hazen. Secretary of War Robert T. Lincoln overruled Hazen and ordered him accepted. After training, Greene served as a weather observer and led the Signal Corps station at Pensacola, Florida.

Codebreaking

Purple Is Broken

On September 27, 1940, Frank Rowlett handed William Friedman two fully decoded messages in Japan's top diplomatic cipher, called Purple. A week earlier Genevieve Grotjan of the Army's Signal Intelligence Service had spotted the key pattern. The intelligence, code-named Magic, flowed through the war.

Also this monthOn September 1, 1905, the Signal School opened at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas, recognizing the Signal Corps as a distinct branch of the Army.
