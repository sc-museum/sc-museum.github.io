This Month in History

Signal Corps anniversaries for the month, with a door into the room that tells each story.

October
Two October 4thsA pigeon carried a Signal Corps message through gunfire in 1918. In 1957 Signal Corps antennas were the first in the U.S. government to record Sputnik.
Oct11890
Weather

The weather service leaves the Army

On October 1, 1890, President Benjamin Harrison signed the law creating the Weather Bureau in the Department of Agriculture. For about twenty years Signal Corps soldiers had run the nation's weather service from posts across the country. The handover took effect on July 1, 1891.

Oct41918
Signal Corps Pigeon Service

Cher Ami and the Lost Battalion

Cut off in the Argonne Forest and shelled by their own artillery, Maj. Charles Whittlesey's men sent a message by Signal Corps pigeon. Cher Ami reached his loft badly wounded, the shelling stopped, and the battalion was relieved on the night of October 7. His mounted remains are now at the Smithsonian.

Oct41957
Signal Corps Laboratories

Sputnik, heard first at Deal

When the Soviet Union launched Sputnik I, Signal Corps engineers at the Deal Test Area, a satellite campus of Fort Monmouth, New Jersey, were the first U.S. government team to detect and record its signals. They followed 273 orbits over about 500 hours, though no project had been set up for the job.

Oct91909
Army Aviation

The first Army pilots learn to fly

On October 9, 1909, formal flight training began at College Park, Maryland, with Wilbur Wright teaching two Army lieutenants. On October 26 Lt. Frederic Humphreys flew solo, and Lt. Frank Lahm followed minutes later. On October 10, 1911, the Army tried dropping bombs from an airplane for the first time.

All monthOct1918
Hello Girls

The switchboards of Meuse-Argonne

During the Meuse-Argonne offensive, Grace Banker's Signal Corps telephone operators worked the lines from Bar-le-Duc, France. At the height of the fighting they connected 150,000 calls a day. The war ended on November 11, 1918, and in 1977 Congress finally gave the women of the Signal Corps veteran status.

Also this monthAmerican Archives Month: the CECOM History Office archive holds more than a hundred years of Signal Corps history, from Greely's Arctic journal to World War I training-camp photographs.
November
Weather, Wings, and CyberNovember runs from the Signal Service's first telegraphed weather reports in 1870 to the first West Point cadets choosing the new Cyber Branch in 2014.
Nov11870
Signal Service Weather

The Army's Weather Network Goes Live

At 7:35 a.m. on November 1, 1870, Signal Service observer-sergeants at 24 stations took the first synchronized weather readings and sent them by telegraph to Washington, D.C. The stations stretched from Boston to Key West and west to Cheyenne in Wyoming Territory. The network, organized under Chief Signal Officer Albert J. Myer, began the Army's twenty years of national weather reporting.

Nov291911
Army Aviation in Augusta

Army Fliers Winter in Augusta

On November 29, 1911, the Signal Corps aviation school from College Park, Maryland, arrived in Augusta, Georgia, to keep flying through the winter on the Barnes farm. That winter proved unusually harsh: heavy snow fell in January and February 1912. Pilots trained here included Henry H. "Hap" Arnold and Thomas DeW. Milling. The Signal Corps returned to the Augusta area in 1948 at Camp Gordon.

Nov81942
World War II

Operation TORCH Tests the Signal Corps

On November 8, 1942, British and American forces landed in North Africa in Operation TORCH, which the Army's branch history calls the Signal Corps' "first major test of the war." Naval gunfire knocked out many shipboard radios, and the heavy SCR-299 radio trucks were stowed so deep in the holds that only one came ashore in time. Two British communications ships helped fill the gap.

Nov241877
Coastal Signal Stations

Telegraph at the Wreck of the Huron

Early on November 24, 1877, the steamer Huron ran aground off Nags Head, North Carolina. Sgt. S. W. Naylor, the Signal Corps observer at Kitty Hawk, eight miles away, hurried to the scene and set up a telegraph station on the shore. It sent more than 550 messages through December 11. Only 34 of the 132 men aboard survived.

Nov81966
Vietnam / Medal of Honor

Capt. Euripides Rubio, Communications Officer

On November 8, 1966, during Operation ATTLEBORO in Tay Ninh Province, Vietnam, Capt. Euripides Rubio was communications officer of the 1st Battalion, 28th Infantry. Wounded three times, he took command of a rifle company, then carried a misplaced marking smoke grenade toward the enemy so the air strikes would fall on the enemy, not his men. He received the Medal of Honor posthumously.

Nov202014
Cyber Branch

First Cadets Branch Cyber

On November 20, 2014, at Branch Night at the U.S. Military Academy, 15 cadets of the Class of 2015 received assignments as Cyber officers (17A). They were the first West Point cadets to branch directly into the new Cyber Branch, created that September 1. The next night they met Army Cyber Command and Cyber School leaders, including Cyber School Commandant Col. Jennifer Buckner.

Also this monthBeginning in November 1917, the Signal Corps recruited women from commercial telephone companies, plus bilingual volunteers, as telephone operators; they became the Signal Corps Female Telephone Operators Unit, better known as the "Hello Girls."
December
From Radar to OrbitSignal Corps radar spotted the attackers approaching Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, and seventeen years later, on December 18, 1958, the Corps launched the world's first communications satellite.
Dec91941
Camp Gordon

Camp Gordon Opens for Business

Two days after Pearl Harbor, on December 9, 1941, camp commander Col. Herbert W. Schmid moved his small staff from a temporary office in Augusta's post office to the unfinished headquarters building at Camp Gordon. That month the 4th Infantry Division began moving in. The camp hosted three divisions in World War II and became a Signal Corps training center in 1948.

Dec71941
World War II / Radar

Radar Warning at Opana

At 7:02 a.m. on December 7, 1941, Signal Corps Pvts. Joseph L. Lockard and George A. Elliott, still running their radar at Opana on Oahu's northern tip after their watch ended, saw a large echo about 130 miles out. They telephoned the information center at Fort Shafter but were told to "Forget it." The attack on Pearl Harbor began at 7:55.

Dec161944
World War II

Holding the Lines in the Bulge

On December 16, 1944, German forces attacked through the Ardennes, opening the Battle of the Bulge. Though American units were caught by surprise, their communications held. Col. Garland C. Black, signal officer of Gen. Omar Bradley's 12th Army Group, had built alternate routes for key circuits, using French underground cables backed by open wire lines. Bradley never lost contact with his armies.

Dec181958
Satellites

SCORE: A Voice from Orbit

On December 18, 1958, the Signal Corps, with an Air Force Atlas rocket, launched Project SCORE, the world's first communications satellite. It showed that an orbiting relay could receive, store, and send on voice and coded signals. SCORE broadcast a taped Christmas greeting from President Eisenhower: "my voice is coming to you from a satellite circling in outer space." It was built to last only weeks.

Dec231907
Army Aviation

The Army Advertises for an Airplane

On December 23, 1907, the Signal Corps issued Specification No. 486, seeking bids for a "heavier-than-air flying machine." It had to carry two people, fly at least 40 miles per hour, and stay aloft for an hour. Bids were due February 1, 1908. The Wright brothers won the contract, and their machine became the Army's first airplane, Signal Corps No. 1.

Also this monthIn December 1936, Signal Corps engineers ran the first field test of their radar at the Newark, New Jersey, airport, where it detected an airplane seven miles away.
January
Moon Echo to CyberJanuary links the Signal Corps' 1946 radar echo from the Moon with Fort Gordon's 2014 start as the Army's Cyber Center of Excellence.
Jan101946
Signal Corps Laboratories

Project Diana Reaches the Moon

On January 10, 1946, a Signal Corps team at the Evans Signal Laboratory in New Jersey bounced a radar signal off the Moon and received its echo about 2.5 seconds later. Led by John H. DeWitt, and drawing on scientist Walter S. McAfee's calculations, the team used a modified SCR-271 radar. Project Diana showed that signals could pass through the ionosphere into space.

All monthJan1871
Signal Service Weather

Cleveland Abbe Joins the Signal Corps

In January 1871 the Signal Corps hired Professor Cleveland Abbe of the Cincinnati Observatory as a weather forecaster. Abbe had issued forecasts in Cincinnati since 1869, and the Corps adopted many of his methods. Though once skeptical of a military weather service, he stayed with it for its full twenty years and founded the Monthly Weather Review.

All monthJan1912
Army Aviation in Augusta

Wilbur Wright Visits Augusta

In January 1912 Wilbur Wright visited the Signal Corps aviation school wintering on the Barnes farm in Augusta, Georgia. His visit came during an unusually harsh winter, with heavy snow in January and February that cut into flying time. The Army's branch history calls him "no doubt a source of much valuable advice." Wright died of typhoid fever that May, at age forty-five.

All monthJan1943
Women in the Signal Corps

WAACs Join the Signal Corps

Beginning in January 1943, the Signal Corps received its first female soldiers, members of the Women's Army Auxiliary Corps, later the Women's Army Corps. At least 5,000 WACs served with the Corps at home and overseas. They worked as message center clerks and switchboard operators, in film libraries and laboratories, and in signal intelligence duties such as cryptography.

Jan242014
Fort Gordon / Cyber

Fort Gordon Becomes the Cyber Center of Excellence

On January 24, 2014, the Army issued an order (EXORD 057-14) establishing the U.S. Army Cyber Center of Excellence and a Cyber School at Fort Gordon. The Signal Center of Excellence became the Cyber Center of Excellence, with the Signal and Cyber Schools under one two-star command. The Army's Cyber Branch followed on September 1, 2014.

Also this monthOn January 4, 2016, the first Cyber Captains Career Course began at the Army Cyber School at Fort Gordon, with twenty officers starting the 26-week course.
February
Weather and WingsIn February 1870 Congress gave the Signal Corps a national weather service, and in February 1908 the Corps signed the Army's first airplane contract.
Feb91870
Weather

Congress Creates a Weather Service

On February 9, 1870, President Ulysses S. Grant signed a joint resolution of Congress directing the Secretary of War to take weather observations at military stations and to warn the Great Lakes and seacoast of approaching storms by telegraph. Chief Signal Officer Albert J. Myer asked for the job, and the Signal Corps took on the work that grew into today's National Weather Service.

Feb41881
Signal Corps founder

Fort Whipple Becomes Fort Myer

On February 4, 1881, the Army renamed Fort Whipple, Virginia, as Fort Myer to honor Albert J. Myer, the Army's first signal officer and founder of the Signal Corps. Signal training had been held at the post. Myer had died the previous August at age 51, soon after his promotion to brigadier general. As head of the weather service, he was popularly known as "Old Probabilities."

Feb101906
Leaders

Greely's Long Watch Ends

On February 10, 1906, President Theodore Roosevelt promoted Chief Signal Officer Adolphus W. Greely to major general, and the War Department sent him to command the Pacific Division at San Francisco. Greely had led the Signal Corps for nearly nineteen years, the longest of any chief signal officer, guiding it from the weather-service years into the electrical age.

Feb101908
Aviation

The Army Orders Its First Airplane

On February 10, 1908, the Signal Corps and Wilbur and Orville Wright signed the first formal Army airplane contract. Under Signal Corps Specification No. 486, the machine had to carry two people at about 40 miles per hour and stay in the air for at least an hour. The price was $25,000; a speed bonus later raised the payment to $30,000.

Feb81968
Fort Gordon

Last Signal OCS Class at Fort Gordon

On February 8, 1968, the 53 members of Class 04-68 took the oath as second lieutenants in Fort Gordon's Alexander Hall, ending the Signal Officer Candidate School. Activated at Fort Gordon in September 1965, the 23-week course had trained more than 2,000 officers. Many graduates soon carried heavy responsibilities in Vietnam, often beyond their rank.

Also this monthIn February 1942 the Signal Corps bought Paramount's film studio in Astoria, Long Island, which opened that May as the Signal Corps Photographic Center.
March
A Corps Takes ShapeMarch marks both the 1863 law that created a Signal Corps for the Civil War and the 1956 order that made Camp Gordon the permanent Fort Gordon.
Mar31863
Civil War

Lincoln Signs the Signal Corps Into Law

On March 3, 1863, President Abraham Lincoln signed a bill that organized a Signal Corps for the duration of the "present rebellion." Until then, Albert J. Myer had depended on officers and men detailed from other units. The law created a chief signal officer with the rank of colonel and required entrance examinations. Historian Rebecca Raines called it "truly a signal victory."

Mar51913
Aviation

From Augusta to the 1st Aero Squadron

In February 1913, trouble with Mexico led the Army to mobilize along the border, and Signal Corps aviators wintering at Augusta, Georgia, were called to Texas. On March 5, 1913, at Texas City, they were organized as the 1st Aero Squadron (Provisional), with nine airplanes, under Capt. Charles deForest Chandler. Augusta's winter flying school later lent its area to Camp Gordon's story.

Mar61918
Women in the Signal Corps

First Women Operators Sail for France

On March 6, 1918, the first unit of 33 Signal Corps women telephone operators sailed for Europe aboard the Army transport Celtic, led by chief operator Grace Banker. Fluent in French and English, they began official duties in France on March 24 and ran American Expeditionary Forces switchboards in Paris, Chaumont, and seventy-five other French cities and towns.

Mar211956
Fort Gordon

Camp Gordon Becomes Fort Gordon

On March 21, 1956, the Army redesignated Camp Gordon, Georgia, as Fort Gordon, making it a permanent installation. Opened as a World War II training camp near Augusta, the post became a Signal Corps training center in 1948, home of the Southeastern Signal School. It grew quickly during the Korean War, when the Army needed many more trained signal soldiers.

Mar241976
Notable soldiers

First Black General of the Signal Corps

On March 24, 1976, Brig. Gen. Emmett Paige Jr. became the Signal Corps' first Black general officer. He had commanded the 361st Signal Battalion in Vietnam and was then leading the 11th Signal Group at Fort Huachuca, Arizona. Paige went on to lead four Army communications and electronics organizations and retired in 1988 after more than 41 years in uniform.

Mar282014
Cyber

Signal Center Becomes Cyber Center

On March 28, 2014, according to the Army Cyber School's chronology, the Signal Center of Excellence at Fort Gordon transitioned to the Cyber Center of Excellence. An Army order that January had created the new center and a Cyber School to stand beside the Signal School. The Army formally established the Cyber Branch on September 1, 2014.

Also this monthOn March 17, 1958, the Navy launched Vanguard I, the first solar-powered satellite, using solar cells developed by the Signal Corps.
April
Keeping Word MovingFrom the San Francisco earthquake of April 1906 to the first weather satellite in April 1960, the Signal Corps kept information flowing when it mattered.
Apr61865
Civil War

The Signal Corps' Civil War Medal of Honor

On April 6, 1865, near Jetersville, Virginia, during Lee's retreat from Petersburg, Pvt. Morgan D. Lane of the Signal Corps helped capture crew members of the Confederate gunboat Nansemond and seized the gunboat's flag. His Medal of Honor, issued in March 1866, was the only one earned by a Civil War Signal Corps soldier. Lee surrendered three days later.

Apr181906
Disaster response

Wires Down in San Francisco

The San Francisco earthquake struck early on April 18, 1906, and fire soon cut the city's last telegraph links to the rest of the country. By about five hours after the quake, Capt. Leonard D. Wildman, the departmental signal officer, had a field telegraph line running from the Presidio to the edge of the fire. Signal Corps operators and equipment then helped commercial companies restore service.

Apr11960
Space and weather

The First Weather Satellite

On April 1, 1960, NASA launched TIROS, the world's first weather satellite. Signal Corps scientists at Fort Monmouth, New Jersey, worked with NASA, the Weather Bureau, RCA, and others on its television camera system, and a ground station at Fort Monmouth received its cloud pictures. TIROS operated for 78 days and showed that satellites could watch the world's weather.

Apr11966
Vietnam

One Brigade for Vietnam's Signals

On April 1, 1966, the Army activated a new signal brigade in Vietnam, soon named the 1st Signal Brigade, to bring nearly all signal units in the country under one command. Led by Robert D. Terry, it was headquartered first at Saigon and later Long Binh. It merged tactical and strategic communications and was the first brigade of its kind in Signal Corps history.

Also this monthOn April 1, 2014, MSG Phillip E. Williams became the first of seven soldiers assigned to work only on building the new Army Cyber School at Fort Gordon.
May
Handing Off, Moving UpIn May 1918 the Signal Corps handed military aviation to the War Department, and in May 2016 Fort Gordon graduated the Army's first class of Cyber officers.
May201918
World War I / Aviation

Aviation Leaves the Signal Corps

On May 20, 1918, the president issued an executive order that fully separated military aviation from the Signal Corps and placed it under the direct control of the Secretary of War. The Division of Military Aeronautics and the Bureau of Aircraft Production became independent agencies within the War Department. The Signal Corps had guided Army aviation through its early years. It kept one part of the work: responsibility for airborne radio.

May11957
Pigeons

The Army's Pigeon Lofts Close

On May 1, 1957, the Signal Corps closed its Pigeon Breeding and Training Branch at Fort Monmouth. Electronics had made the Army's message-carrying birds obsolete, much as progress had earlier retired the Army's horses and mules. The Corps sold its pigeons to the public. Its remaining war heroes, such as G.I. Joe, were presented to zoos around the country instead.

May131968
Vietnam

Attack on Black Virgin Mountain

On the night of May 13, 1968, Viet Cong forces attacked a remote Signal Corps site atop Nui Ba Den, or Black Virgin Mountain, near Tay Ninh in South Vietnam. Twenty-three communicators were killed, and most of their equipment was destroyed. Pfc. Thomas M. Torma of the 86th Signal Battalion received the Silver Star for his heroism while defending the site. It was one of the worst attacks on signal soldiers of the war.

May151984
Information age

Army Information Systems Command

On May 15, 1984, the Army Communications Command became the Army Information Systems Command. The change brought communications, automation, visual information, publications and printing, and records management under one headquarters, an approach the Army called the Information Mission Area. The new command also took in the Army Computer Systems Command. Lt. Gen. Clarence E. McKnight Jr. organized it.

May262016
Cyber / Fort Gordon

First Cyber Officers Graduate

On May 26, 2016, twenty-one officers graduated at Fort Gordon as the first class of the Cyber Basic Officer Leader Course. Their ten months of training had begun on August 6, 2015, less than a year after the Army established its Cyber branch. Col. Jennifer Buckner, the first commandant of the U.S. Army Cyber School, addressed the graduates.

Also this monthIn May 1944 the Signal Corps sent a nine-word test message around the world over the Army Command and Administrative Network (ACAN) in three and one-half minutes; a similar test a year later took nine and a half seconds.
June
Happy Birthday, Signal CorpsThe branch born on June 21, 1860, made Fort Gordon its regimental home on June 1, 1986.
Jun211860
Founding

The Signal Corps Is Born

On June 21, 1860, President James Buchanan signed an Army appropriations bill that added one signal officer to the Army staff. With that act the U.S. Army Signal Corps was born, making the U.S. Army the first in the world with a dedicated communications branch. On June 27 the Senate confirmed Dr. Albert J. Myer, an Army surgeon and inventor of the 'wigwag' flag system, as signal officer with the rank of major.

Jun221884
Arctic / Weather science

Greely Found at Cape Sabine

On June 22, 1884, a rescue expedition under Capt. Winfield Scott Schley found 1st Lt. Adolphus W. Greely of the Signal Corps at Cape Sabine in the Arctic. Greely's party had gone to Lady Franklin Bay in 1881 to make weather and other scientific observations as part of an international polar research effort. Resupply efforts had failed. Only Greely and six others were still alive, and one of them died soon after.

Jun61944
World War II / Combat camera

First Pictures from Omaha Beach

On June 6, 1944, Capt. Herman V. Wall commanded the 165th Signal Photographic Company, First Army's photo unit, as its teams covered the Normandy landings on both American beaches. Wall photographed the assault on Omaha Beach himself and was seriously wounded; he later lost his left leg. He still made sure his film reached England for processing. His were the first pictures received of the actual landings.

Jun11986
Fort Gordon

Fort Gordon Becomes Regimental Home

On June 1, 1986, the Signal Corps Regiment was established under the U.S. Army Regimental System, with Fort Gordon as its regimental home base. The whole branch formed one regiment, and every soldier trained in a Signal specialty joined it on graduating from the branch school. Two days later the commander of the Signal Center and Fort Gordon also became chief of signal. Maj. Gen. Thurman D. Rodgers was the first.

Jun102025
Fort Gordon

A Medal of Honor Namesake

On June 10, 2025, the Army announced that the post would carry the name Fort Gordon in honor of Master Sgt. Gary I. Gordon. He received the Medal of Honor, posthumously, for his valor during the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu, Somalia. The installation had first taken the Gordon name as Camp Gordon at its groundbreaking in October 1941.

July
Eyes Above the BattleFrom a signal station on Little Round Top in July 1863 to a balloon over Santiago in July 1898, the Signal Corps watched the battlefield from above and reported what it saw.
Jul21863
Civil War

Signals from Little Round Top

At Gettysburg, signalmen were the first Union troops to occupy Little Round Top. Just before noon on July 2, 1863, the station warned army headquarters that one mile west, "the woods are full of them." That afternoon Capt. James S. Hall signaled that a heavy Confederate column was moving against the Union left. The column's slow countermarch gave Maj. Gen. George G. Meade time to send troops.

Jul11898
Spanish-American War

A Balloon over San Juan Hill

On July 1, 1898, during the battle for Santiago, Cuba, a Signal Corps balloon under Maj. Joseph Maxfield went up with Lt. Col. George F. Derby aboard. Ordered close to the front, it drew Spanish fire and was torn apart. Still, the observers reported strong Spanish defenses on San Juan Hill and found an unknown trail that sped American troops toward the hill.

Jul11891
Weather

The Weather Service Leaves the Army

On July 1, 1891, the nation's weather service left the Signal Corps. Under a law passed the year before, it moved to the Department of Agriculture as a civilian agency, renamed the Weather Bureau. The Signal Corps had run the service for about two decades. Its enlisted weather observers could leave the Army and join the new bureau, and the Corps shrank to nine officers and fifty sergeants plus the chief signal officer.

Jul181914
Aviation

The Aviation Section Is Created

On July 18, 1914, Congress created the Aviation Section of the Signal Corps, with up to 60 officers and 260 enlisted men. For the first time, the Army had soldiers permanently assigned to aviation duties. The law limited aviation officers to unmarried lieutenants no older than thirty. The section was responsible for operating all military aircraft, including balloons and airplanes.

Jul11974
Fort Gordon

Home of the Signal Corps

On July 1, 1974, as the Army consolidated most of its signal training at Fort Gordon, the post's Southeastern Signal School was renamed the U.S. Army Signal School. The school at Fort Monmouth became the Communications-Electronics School. Three months later, on October 1, Fort Gordon became the U.S. Army Signal Center and Fort Gordon, the new "home of the Signal Corps."

Jul242020
Cyber / Fort Gordon

Army Cyber Command Arrives

On July 24, 2020, U.S. Army Cyber Command uncased its colors at Fortitude Hall, its new headquarters at Fort Gordon. Created in October 2010, the command had been based in the Washington, D.C., area, with elements at Fort Belvoir and Fort Meade. Its commander, Lt. Gen. Stephen G. Fogarty, said the ceremony symbolized "the permanent establishment of ARCYBER headquarters at Fort Gordon."

Also this monthOn July 30, 1909, Orville Wright and 1st Lt. Benjamin D. Foulois flew the Signal Corps' ten-mile speed test between Fort Myer and Alexandria, Virginia, watched by about seven thousand people, including President Taft.
August
Signals Outrun SilenceIn August 1898 American troops fought at Manila because word of a cease-fire arrived too late; in August 1964 the Army opened the first satellite link in a combat zone.
Aug141861
Civil War

A Telegraph Train for the Army

On August 14, 1861, Assistant Secretary of War Thomas A. Scott authorized Albert J. Myer, founder of the Signal Corps, to buy a small telegraph train. The "train" was a set of horse-drawn wagons carrying telegraph sets, reels of insulated wire, and iron lances for stringing temporary "flying telegraph lines." Electric signals were faster than Myer's flags and torches, which averaged about three words per minute.

Aug131898
Spanish-American War

The Battle Fought After the Peace

On August 13, 1898, American troops fought the Battle of Manila, unaware that a cease-fire had been signed the day before. Manila's cable had been cut, so the news arrived days late. Signal detachments served with each division and brigade commander, ran insulated wire along the beach as troops advanced, and used flags to direct Navy gunfire against Spanish positions.

Aug151935
Alaska / Notable soldier

News from the Top of the World

On August 15, 1935, humorist Will Rogers and aviator Wiley Post died when their plane crashed about fifteen miles south of Point Barrow, Alaska. Sgt. Stanley R. Morgan, the Signal Corps radio operator at Barrow, learned of the crash from a Native runner and went to the scene. Returning to his station, he signaled news of the tragedy to the world.

All monthAug1964
Satellites / Vietnam

First Satellite Link in a Combat Zone

In August 1964, after the Tonkin Gulf incidents exposed weak radio links between Saigon and Washington, the Army rushed an experimental SYNCOM satellite ground terminal to Vietnam. Run by a team under Warrant Officer Jack H. Inman, it went into service late that month, giving one telephone and one teletype circuit to Hawaii. It was the first use of satellite communications in a combat zone.

Aug42014
Cyber / Fort Gordon

The Army Cyber School Opens

On August 4, 2014, the U.S. Army Cyber School headquarters was unveiled at Fort Gordon by Maj. Gen. LaWarren Patterson, commander of the Cyber Center of Excellence, and Col. Jennifer Buckner was announced as its first commandant. A year later, on August 6, 2015, 31 newly commissioned lieutenants began the first Cyber Basic Officer Leader Course at the school.

Also this monthAlbert J. Myer, founder of the Signal Corps and builder of its weather service, died on August 24, 1880, at age 51.
September
Flags to FirewallsIn September 1862 signalmen on Elk Mountain flagged warnings across the Antietam battlefield; in September 2014 the Army created its Cyber branch to fight in a new domain.
Sep171862
Civil War

Warning from Elk Mountain

At the Battle of Antietam on September 17, 1862, a Union signal station on Elk Mountain overlooked the battlefield. The party under Lt. Joseph Gloskoski spotted A. P. Hill's Confederate division arriving from Harpers Ferry and flagged a warning to General Burnside to watch his left. Burnside was away from his signal station when the message came, and Hill's counterattack drove his troops back.

Sep261884
Notable soldier / Weather

W. Hallett Greene Joins the Signal Corps

On September 26, 1884, W. Hallett Greene became the first Black soldier admitted to the Signal Corps. A new graduate of the College of the City of New York, he had been rejected by Chief Signal Officer William B. Hazen. Secretary of War Robert T. Lincoln overruled Hazen and ordered him accepted. After training, Greene served as a weather observer and led the Signal Corps station at Pensacola, Florida.

Sep121918
World War I

Signal Troops at St. Mihiel

On September 12, 1918, the new U.S. First Army attacked the St. Mihiel salient in France. Signal troops worked to keep units connected. Men of the 55th Telegraph and 317th Field Signal Battalions dug a cable trench six feet deep and one kilometer long through nearly solid rock, without explosives. Six women telephone operators served at First Army headquarters. By September 16 the salient was cleared.

Sep201965
Fort Gordon / Vietnam era

Signal Officer Candidate School at Fort Gordon

On September 20, 1965, an order activated the Signal Officer Candidate School at Fort Gordon, creating the 1st Officer Candidate Battalion under Maj. John J. McManus. The program began with ten officers and no facilities of its own. The first class arrived that November. When the last class was commissioned in February 1968, the school had produced 2,210 second lieutenants during the Vietnam War era.

Sep12014
Cyber

The Cyber Branch Is Born

On September 1, 2014, the Army's Cyber branch was officially established. Secretary of the Army John McHugh had signed General Orders 2014-63 on August 21, and it took effect on the first of September, weeks after the Army Cyber School headquarters was unveiled at Fort Gordon. In 2016 the U.S. Army Center of Military History designated September 1 as the Cyber Corps birthday.

Sep32020
Cyber / Fort Gordon

Army Cyber Command Dedicates Fortitude Hall

On September 3, 2020, U.S. Army Cyber Command dedicated Fortitude Hall, its new headquarters at Fort Gordon. Lt. Gen. Stephen G. Fogarty, Command Sgt. Maj. Sheryl D. Lyon, and deputy Ron Pontius unveiled the dedication plaque. The move brought the command's headquarters from the Washington, D.C., area to Georgia. Staff had begun working in the 336,000-square-foot building in July 2020.

Also this monthOn September 1, 1905, the Signal School opened at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas, recognizing the Signal Corps as a distinct branch of the Army.
