The road so far

From a Facebook group of Signal veterans to a society with a building in its sights. Press reports are linked in .

  1. 8 June 2020
    The Society gathers online

    Brigadier General Welton Chase, Jr. opens the Fort Gordon Historical Museum Society's member group with its mission: raise funds to acquire, build and run a museum near Fort Gordon, and keep the Signal Corps Museum Collection in Augusta. Its first request to members: "Please share your photos and history here."

  2. 21 June 2020
    The Signal Corps turns 160

    Members mark the Regiment's birthday, 160 years after Congress created the Signal Corps on 21 June 1860.

  3. August 2020
    A four-phase plan

    The Society publishes its road map: (1) set up the framework for success, (2) raise initial funding and select a facility, (3) establish the museum and transfer the artifacts, (4) cut the ribbon and open.

  4. September 2020
    The first fact sheets

    One-page histories of Signal Corps figures begin with Theodor "Dr. Seuss" Geisel and Frank Capra, both of whom served in the Signal Corps in the Second World War. Read more in .

  5. October 2020
    Heritage is born

    The first issue of the Society's magazine leads with the Academy Awards won by Signal Corps filmmakers. Read issue 1.

  6. 25 February 2021
    The Signal Corps Museum closes

    Base construction takes the museum's building, and its colors are cased at a ceremony on post. About 4,100 artifacts must leave the building.

  7. March 2021
    Heritage issue 2

    The Military Police School at Camp Gordon. Read issue 2.

  8. April 2021
    A building in sight

    The Society identifies a new home just outside Gate 1: the former National Science Center building at Gordon Highway and Craig Sims Parkway.

  9. May 2021
    Fireside Chats

    LTG John B. Morrison Jr., the Army's Deputy Chief of Staff, G-6, joins the Society's Fireside Chat series. Watch it in the .

  10. May 2021
    The collection goes into storage

    A Center of Military History team packs the collection with the museum's curator. Most of it moves to Anniston, Alabama, to be held until it has a new home.

  11. September 2021
    Heritage issue 3

    Special operations communicators. Read issue 3.

  12. February 2022
    $125,000 raised

    The Augusta Press reports the Society has raised $125,000 toward the roughly $4 million needed to buy, renovate and staff the building.

  13. October 2023
    About half a million raised

    At a donation breakfast, the Society reports about $500,000 raised to buy a building for the artifacts held at Anniston Army Depot.

  14. 27 October 2023
    Fort Gordon becomes Fort Eisenhower

    The post is redesignated for General of the Army Dwight D. Eisenhower. The museum's history keeps the name Fort Gordon wherever the record uses it.

  15. 2024
    Signal & Cyber Corps Museum Society

    The Society takes its present name, covering the Cyber Corps as well as the Signal Regiment.

  16. 11 June 2025
    Fort Gordon again

    The post's name returns to Fort Gordon, now honoring Master Sgt. Gary I. Gordon, Medal of Honor.
