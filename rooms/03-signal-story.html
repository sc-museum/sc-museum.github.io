The Signal Story

How a Civil War signal detail became the Army's nerve system — and how Fort Gordon became its home.

From flags to fiber

The U.S. Army Signal Corps was established on June 21, 1860, when Congress created the position of Signal Officer and Major Albert J. Myer began organizing a dedicated communications service for the Army. Myer's wig-wag flag signaling, developed in the years before the Civil War, gave field commanders something they had never reliably had: the ability to pass orders and observations across a battlefield faster than a rider could carry them.

Over the following century the Corps carried that mission through every generation of technology it touched — telegraph and heliograph in the Indian Wars, and radio experiments in the 1900s and 1910s, battlefield telephone and teletype in two World Wars, satellite and microwave systems in the Cold War, and today's data networks and cyber operations. Each generation of Signaleers built on the last.

Fort Gordon's own role in that story began after the Second World War. Camp Gordon, built in 1941 as a division training post, was nearly deactivated when the war ended — but in 1948 the Army activated the Signal Corps Training Center there. The post was made permanent and renamed Fort Gordon in March 1956, and in June 1986 the Army established the U.S. Army Signal Corps Regiment with Fort Gordon as its regimental home. It has trained Signal Soldiers ever since.

Signal Guidons

The guidon — a small swallow-tailed flag — has marked Signal units on the march since the horse-cavalry era. Each color and device identifies a unit's branch and lineage at a glance.

Two regiments, one lineage

Today Fort Gordon is regimental home to both the Signal Corps and the Army's Cyber branch — sister disciplines built on the same founding idea: whoever controls the flow of information shapes the fight.

Signal Corps

Est. 1860. Builds, operates, and defends the Army's communications networks — from tactical radio nets to strategic satellite links.

Cyber Regiment

The Army's newest branch, headquartered alongside Signal at Fort Gordon's Cyber Center of Excellence — offense, defense, and information warfare in the network domain.
