Signal Regiment · since 1986 Distinguished Members of the Signal Regiment 29 members listed · classes of 2023–2025 · 4 profiles

The Regiment’s own honor for those whose service left a lasting mark on the Signal Corps, the Army and the Nation.

When the Signal Corps entered the Army Regimental System on June 1, 1986, it created a way to honor its own: the Distinguished Members of the Regiment. Each year the Chief of Signal inducts Soldiers and Army civilians whose service left a lasting mark on the Regiment, the Army and the Nation. Some are honored after their deaths.

Membership is permanent, and there is no limit on how many may be named. The honor is mostly ceremonial. Its purpose is to keep the Regiment’s history and traditions alive and to link today’s Signal Soldiers with those who came before them. Each inductee receives a lapel pin and a certificate while the story of their service is read aloud. In recent years the ceremony has been held in the Augusta area each June, and in 2025 it opened the Signal Corps Anniversary Ball. Close to 200 people have been inducted since 1986.

Profiles From the Signal rolls

Four Distinguished Members whose stories reach across the Regiment’s history.

Gen. Dennis L. Via
Distinguished Member · 2012

In 2012 Dennis L. Via became the first Signal Corps officer in the Regiment’s 152-year history to reach four-star rank. On December 12 of that year, the Chief of Signal, Maj. Gen. LaWarren V. Patterson, inducted him as a Distinguished Member of the Regiment at the Signal Corps Museum at Fort Gordon. About 25 generals were present for the annual Signal Corps general officer and senior enlisted conference. Patterson joined him as a Distinguished Member in 2024.

Fort Gordon Public Affairs, 2012 ↗

Charles J. Chibitty
Comanche code talker · Distinguished Member · 2025

In December 1940 the Army recruited 17 Comanche men to serve as code talkers in the 4th Signal Company. Speaking their own language, they passed messages by radio and telephone that the enemy could not read. Charles J. Chibitty, a radio operator, landed at Utah Beach in Normandy in June 1944. His first message ashore went out in Comanche to another code talker on an incoming boat. Fourteen Comanche code talkers landed that day. Two were wounded, and all of them survived.

Recognition came late. France honored the code talkers of both world wars in 1989. In November 1999 Chibitty, by then the last surviving Comanche code talker, was honored at the Pentagon. He died on July 20, 2005. On June 28, 2025, the Signal Regiment inducted him as a Distinguished Member, posthumously.

Maj. Gen. George Owen Squier
Chief Signal Officer, 1917 · Distinguished Member · 2023

George Owen Squier (1865–1934) graduated from West Point in 1887 and became one of the Army’s leading scientists. He was named Chief Signal Officer in February 1917, as the United States entered the First World War, and was responsible for both Army communications and Army aviation. Within a year the Air Service grew under him from 65 officers and 1,100 men to 12,000 officers and 135,000 men. He also decided to create the Signal Corps laboratories at what became Fort Monmouth, New Jersey, and received the Distinguished Service Medal for his wartime service. The Regiment inducted him as a Distinguished Member, posthumously, in 2023.

Lt. Col. William Wyler
Filmmaker · Distinguished Member · 2025

William Wyler, the Academy Award-winning Hollywood director, made combat documentaries during the Second World War. The best known, The Memphis Belle: A Story of a Flying Fortress (1944), was filmed aboard B-17 bombers over Europe, and Wyler flew combat missions to make it. The Signal Regiment inducted Lt. Col. Wyler as a Distinguished Member, posthumously, on June 28, 2025.

Cyber Corps · since 2014 Distinguished and Honorary Members of the Cyber Corps 10 members listed · 7 Distinguished · 3 Honorary

The Cyber Corps was created on September 1, 2014, the Army’s first new branch since Special Forces in 1987, and it keeps its own version of the tradition under Army Regulation 870-21. Distinguished Members must have served in the Cyber Corps, in the Regular Army, the National Guard or the Army Reserve. Honorary Members do not need to have served in the Corps or in the Army at all; Soldiers, civilians and spouses can all be named. Both are lifetime honors.

The Corps also appoints three retired leaders as its Honorary Officer, Honorary Warrant Officer and Honorary Sergeant Major of the Corps, each for a three-year term. For 2024 to 2027 they are Col. (Ret.) John D. Branch, Chief Warrant Officer 5 (Ret.) Mark A. Mollenkopf and Command Sgt. Maj. (Ret.) James M. Krog.
